Structure (the formula measured from a high-performing reference):
1. hook (≤ 2 lines, first line ≤ 12 words): an imperative or direct question that makes the viewer physically check or picture something. No greetings, no "in this video".
2. stakes: why it's universal or matters to the viewer.
3. twist (musicCue: "drop"): the counter-intuitive reveal. Start it with a turn word such as "Except", "But", "Here's the thing".
4. history and/or mechanism: how it came to be and how it actually works. Use "Back in <year>" style anchors that the editor turns into typewriter cards.
5. proof: 1–3 modern, relatable examples (devices, brands, daily habits).
6. payoff: the emotional close that reframes the hook.
7. cta: tie the call to action to the topic's metaphor (e.g. "Your power button lies to you. The subscribe button won't."). When the topic has two real sides (a debate people already have, like milk first or tea first), end on a question that asks the viewer to pick one instead; a question gets more comments than "follow".

Rules:
- headline: ≤ 6 words, ≤ 40 characters, a claim rather than a question, title case.
- Every line: one sentence, ≤ 22 words, spoken English (contractions OK), numbers written as digits.
- Put list items in ONE line separated by commas ("Phones, laptops, consoles, the TV remote on your couch.") so the editor can cut a fast montage on each item.
- claimIds: every line that states a fact lists the claim ids it relies on. Lines without facts (hook, cta) may have none.
- emphasis: 1–2 words per line that the captions should highlight.
- Insert a new "turn" word at least every 20–30 seconds (Except, Then, Today, By <year>, Which means).
- Total words within ±10 % of {{wordBudget}}.
- Line ids: l1, l2, …; beat ids: b1, b2, …
